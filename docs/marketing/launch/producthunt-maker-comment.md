# Maker's Comment — Ryan Buck

---

I don't remember what my grandmother sounded like.

She passed when I was young, and somewhere between the funeral and all the years since, her voice just… faded. No recordings. No voicemails. Nothing to play back on a quiet afternoon when I wanted to hear her laugh again. That absence has stayed with me — and it's why I built Heard Again.

Heard Again is an open-source platform for preserving family voices and stories, but the philosophy behind it matters more than the code. This isn't about using AI to simulate someone who's gone. It's about giving families a careful, private place to keep what already belongs to them — recordings of grandma telling her favorite recipe, dad's bedtime stories, the way your partner says "I love you." Voice cloning is there as a preservation tool: capture a voice sample today so your children and grandchildren can hear it decades from now. Everything is consent-first. Everything is owned by your family, stored in your family's archive — not mined, not resold, not fed into someone else's model.

The project is fully open source (MIT license) and self-hostable for free, because I believe tools this personal shouldn't require trusting a stranger's server. If self-hosting isn't your thing, we offer cloud plans starting at $4.99/month — but the code is always yours to inspect, fork, and run wherever you want. What's next: collaborative family scrapbooks, smarter transcription, and integrations that make recording as natural as a phone call. I'd love your feedback, your feature ideas, and — most of all — I hope it inspires you to record someone you love this week.
