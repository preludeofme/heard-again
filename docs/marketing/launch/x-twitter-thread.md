# Heard Again — X/Twitter Launch Thread

## Tweet 1 (Hook)
I can't remember what my grandmother's laugh sounded like.

That sentence stopped me cold. Thousands of photos. Zero recordings. Her voice is just... gone.

So I spent a year building something to make sure that doesn't happen to anyone else.

## Tweet 2 (The Problem)
Here's what I realized: we document everything now. Photos, videos, texts. But almost nobody thinks to record VOICES.

Your mom's storytelling cadence. Your dad's terrible jokes. The way your grandpa says "I love you."

Those are the first things we lose.

## Tweet 3 (What I Built)
I built Heard Again — an open-source platform for preserving family voices and stories.

Record conversations. Upload old audio. Transcribe everything. Build voice profiles of the people you love.

And it all stays with your family. Forever.

## Tweet 4 (Why Open Source)
This had to be open source. Here's why:

Your grandmother's voice recordings don't belong on some startup's server. They belong to your family.

Self-host it. Own your data. Nobody can take it away, shut it down, or monetize it without your permission.

## Tweet 5 (Consent First)
Voice AI makes people nervous. Rightfully so.

So we built consent into everything from day one. Every recording has a verified consent trail. Families control who can access what. You can delete anything, anytime.

Technology should serve families — not the other way around.

## Tweet 6 (What It Does)
The platform gives you:
🎙️ Voice Lab — preserve and train voice profiles
📖 Family Scrapbook — stories, photos, timelines
📝 Smart Transcription — searchable family history
👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Collaborative Memories — build together
🔒 Private Archive — everything encrypted, you hold the keys

## Tweet 7 (The Stack / For Devs)
Built with Next.js, TypeScript, Prisma, PostgreSQL.

MIT licensed. Docker support. Self-host on a Raspberry Pi or run in the cloud.

GitHub: github.com/preludeofme/heard-again
Live: heardagain.com

## Tweet 8 (CTA)
If you've ever thought "I should record Mom telling that story" — today is the day.

Start with one conversation. One voice. One story.

Your family will thank you in 50 years.
