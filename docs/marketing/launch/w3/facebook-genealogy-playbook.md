# Facebook genealogy groups — playbook

These groups are tight communities with long memories and active moderators. Most of them
ban self-promotion outright. One bad post gets you removed and remembered.

The plan is: join, be useful for two weeks, then mention the project once — and only if
someone asks a question it genuinely answers.

## Warm-up — Tue 2026-10-06 to Sun 2026-10-19

Minimum before any mention is allowed:

- 14 days of membership.
- 10+ helpful comments across the groups, on other people's problems.
- 0 posts about Heard Again. 0 links to heardagain.com. 0 mentions of building anything.

What "helpful" means here. These groups answer research questions, so answer research
questions:

- Transcribe a hard-to-read handwritten record for someone.
- Point someone at the right FamilySearch or state archive collection.
- Explain a date convention, a Soundex spelling, a naming pattern.
- Audio and file questions are the natural fit: converting cassettes, cleaning up noisy
  recordings, what file format to keep an archive in, how to back it up. Answer these
  completely and without naming the product. This is also how people learn you know this
  subject, which is what makes a later mention land.

Do not comment "great post". Do not react-farm. Moderators see it.

## The one mention

Allowed from **Sun 2026-10-25**, and only when all of these are true:

1. Someone else asked a question about recording, preserving, or storing family voices.
2. You are giving a real answer, and the project is one line inside it, not the point.
3. You disclose that you built it, in the same sentence.
4. The group's rules do not ban it. If unsure, message the admin first and say what you
   want to post.

If no such question appears during the window, post nothing. There is no deadline.
An unused mention costs nothing. A bad one costs the account.

### Draft — in reply to "how do I record my grandparent / preserve their voice?"

> The gear matters less than people think — the phone in your pocket is fine. Two things
> that made the difference for me:
>
> Don't call it an interview. Ask about one specific memory, and ask permission to record
> so you don't lose the details. "Could you tell me how you and grandpa met? Mind if I
> record it?" People relax about a minute in, and that is when the real story starts.
>
> Then decide where it lives before you record, not after. A phone's voice memos app is
> where recordings go to be forgotten when the phone gets replaced. Anywhere with a folder
> structure and a backup is better.
>
> Full disclosure, that second problem is the one I ended up building a tool for — it's
> called Heard Again, it's open source and free to run on your own machine. Happy to say
> more if it's useful, but honestly the recording is the part that matters. Do that first.

### Draft — in reply to "what do I do with a box of old cassettes?"

> Digitize before you organize. Tape degrades, and the sorting can happen on a copy.
>
> A USB cassette deck is around $30, or a local library or historical society may have
> one you can book. Capture to WAV, not MP3 — keep the lossless file as your master and
> make MP3s for sharing. Label as you go, even badly; "unknown tape 3, woman's voice,
> mentions Akron" is worth far more than "tape 3".
>
> Two copies in two places, one of them not in your house.
>
> I build an open-source thing in this space (Heard Again) so take that with the
> appropriate pinch of salt, but for a one-time box of tapes you don't need any software
> beyond what captures the audio. Get them off the tape first.

### Draft — in reply to "is AI voice cloning of a dead relative creepy?"

Read the room before answering this one. If the thread is already an argument, stay out.

> I think it depends entirely on who it's for and who agreed to it.
>
> Cloning a voice to make a dead person say new things they never said — that is a
> different act from keeping a recording of what they did say. The first one invents.
> The second one preserves. Families seem to feel that difference immediately, even when
> it's hard to put into words.
>
> I work on this (open source project called Heard Again), and the line we hold is
> consent from the living person, recorded with them, in their lifetime. If that's not
> there, the answer is no. I'd rather lose the feature than get this wrong.

## Rules

- Ryan posts. Agents never post, comment, or message in these groups.
- Never post anything about grief that Ryan has not read first.
- One mention per group. Ever. Not one per month.
- If a moderator pushes back, apologise once, delete the comment, do not explain yourself
  at length, and never mention the project in that group again.
- Never post in a group on the same day as a Reddit post. Spread it out.
- Never ask the group to sign up, star the repo, or upvote anything.
