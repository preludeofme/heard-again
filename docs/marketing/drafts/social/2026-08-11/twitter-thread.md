# Twitter Thread: "How to Preserve Family Memories Digitally (Without Losing What Matters)"

---

**1/6**

My grandmother kept a shoebox. Letters, faded photos, and a cassette tape labeled "Christmas 1974."

Nobody in the family could play it for years. When we finally found a working deck, we heard her laugh, my dad as a teenager, dishes clattering in the background.

Twenty-three minutes. Priceless.

**2/6**

We almost lost those 23 minutes because nobody thought to check the shoebox.

Digital memories are actually *more* fragile than that cassette tape was. Hard drives die in 3–5 years. SSDs can lose data in months if they sit unpowered. Platforms shut down and take everything with them.

The "sort later" folder is a graveyard.

**3/6**

Three copies, two different media types, one stored offsite.

That's the rule archivists live by. Computer + external drive + cloud. Offsite means a different building — not just a different folder.

Test your backup once a year. A backup you haven't tested is a wish.

**4/6**

Start with what's already in front of you. Pick ONE shoebox or folder. Spend an hour. Label things while the people who remember are still around.

And use your phone to record voices. Ask one question next time you're with a parent or grandparent. Just hit record.

Photos show what someone looked like. Voices show who they *were*.

**5/6**

A few things that actually help:

Save audio as WAV or FLAC, photos as TIFF, video as MP4 H.264 — not whatever your app exports by default

Name files so a stranger could understand them in 50 years (not IMG_4729.jpg)

Toss a README.txt in each folder explaining who's who

Keep one "Family Archive" folder, synced to cloud, three subfolders: Audio / Photos / Documents

Five minutes a week. Share it with family so it stops being a chore and starts being something you do together.

**6/6**

That shoebox held 23 minutes of a life. We got lucky.

The next generation shouldn't have to get lucky. They should just press play.
